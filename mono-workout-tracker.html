How Mono Lift works

A logbook built around one question: is it time to add weight?

The add-weight cue

This is the whole point of the tool. An exercise shows ↑ ADD WEIGHT when, in your last session, every set reached the top of its target rep range and none of them was logged at RIR 0 (i.e. you didn't grind to failure to get there). Hit the top on some sets but not all, and you'll see ALMOST — stay at this weight one more session.

RIR — reps in reserve

Optional, but it makes the cue smarter. Log how many reps you could still have done: leave 1–2 reps in reserve on working sets. RIR 0 means you went to failure. Leave the field blank if you'd rather not think about it — the cue still works off reps alone.

Target rep ranges

Main compounds default to 6–8, accessories to 8–12. Both are editable per exercise in Settings → Edit program, along with day names and exercise order.

Estimated 1RM

Each lift's detail shows an estimated one-rep max using the Epley formula (weight × (1 + reps/30)). It's a rough comparison tool across different rep counts — not a number to go test.

Where you're training

Pick Gym, Apartment or Home at the top of the logger. Anything the place doesn't have gets swapped for the best alternative you can do there — a barbell bench becomes a DB press, then a push-up — and anything with no substitute is greyed out. Each implement keeps its own weights and its own add-weight cue, because 135 on a bar and 50s in your hands aren't the same number. Correct what each place has in Settings → Edit program.

The Home day

A pull-up bar and the floor is a real session: chin-ups, push-ups, inverted rows, bench dips, split squats, single-leg RDLs, a hollow hold. The best workout is the one that actually happens — a home day beats a skipped gym day every time.

Rest days are the plan

Wednesday is cardio and joints; weekends are off. Nothing here tracks streaks or scolds you for a day away — recovery is when the work actually lands.

Tap an exercise name

The name of any exercise — in the logger or the Lifts tab — opens how to do it: a demo photo, a link to video demonstrations, setup and execution cues, and the mistake people most often make. Its history and chart are on the same card. Tapping the weight/reps fields never opens it, so it can't cover what you're typing.

Nothing you type is lost

Numbers you enter are kept the moment you type them — per day. Switch days, change tabs, close the app, or reload the page and they're still there; a dot on a day chip marks one holding unsaved work. They clear only when you tap Save session for that day, or deliberately discard them. Drafts live on this device only and are never synced.

Sync

Same shared login as the rest of the toolkit — set it once in the Tool Center and this app picks it up. It writes data/mono-workout.json. A half-finished session is kept on this device only and never synced.

Edit program

Rename days, reorder or remove exercises, and set each target rep range (like 6-8). History follows the exercise, so renaming is safe.
